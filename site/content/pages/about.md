Stark Quantum is written by Christian Stark. It is a small independent
project: one person reading primary sources and writing down what they
actually say. There is no newsroom behind it, no vendor paying for it, and
nothing on the site for sale.

The tagline is *quantum, without the press release*. That is the whole
editorial position.

Christian Stark, in Aachen, Germany.

An independent researcher, with no affiliation to any vendor or institution.

sources. Quantum computing is the part of that subject this site covers.

What that does not make me. I am not a lab, not a vendor, and not a

## What this site is trying to do

Quantum computing produces an unusual amount of noise per unit of result. A
laboratory posts a preprint with a careful set of caveats; a week later the
same experiment is described in public as a machine that will break
encryption. Somewhere between those two descriptions the useful information
disappears.

The work here is narrow: read the primary source, separate the claim from the
evidence, and write down what is still missing. That is it. Three sentences
recur often enough that they may as well be stated up front.

- A demo is not a machine.
- A physical qubit is not a logical qubit.
- A press release is not evidence.

None of those are cynical. They are the difference between a result that is
real and a result that is finished, and that difference is where nearly every
misreading of this field begins.

## Why the gap between a demo and a machine is worth tracking

A demonstration proves that something can happen once, under conditions the
experimenters chose, with the errors either post-selected away or absorbed by
classical processing afterwards. A machine is something you can hand to
somebody who did not build it, who then gets a correct answer to a problem
they picked, without knowing the answer in advance.

The distance between those two things is not a matter of scaling up. It runs
through error correction, and error correction has its own thresholds,
overheads, and failure modes. A processor with a thousand physical qubits and
no working error correction is not a thousandth of the way to a useful machine.
It may be a long way from the road entirely.

That gap is the subject of this site. Watching it is more informative than
watching qubit counts, because the count is the number a company can most
easily make go up.

## What is here

- **[Essays](/articles/).** Longer pieces in four categories: Foundations, Hardware and
  error, Tooling, and Applications and limits. Each one ends with the same four
  lines — the claim, the evidence, the missing piece, and why it matters.
  Foundation essays are living documents; they carry a reviewed date separate
  from the date they were first published.
- **[Briefs](/news/).** Two to four sentences tied to a primary source, published only
  when a result carries a number worth reading. Four to six in a week is a busy
  week. Empty weeks are common and are not a failure, and the archive stays
  empty rather than carrying placeholders.
- **The [Reality Board](/reality/).** Four questions about whether this field has arrived,
  with the honest answer to each and a written condition for changing it.
- **A [glossary](/glossary/).** Working definitions aimed at reading announcements rather
  than at passing an exam.
- **A [sources page](/sources/).** The complete allowlist this site reads from, and the
  categories it refuses on principle.

The publication language is English, and only English. The Impressum and the
privacy notice carry German because German law requires it. Nothing else is
translated, and there is no plan to translate the essays.

## What this site is not

It is not a business. It is not a shop and not a consulting practice, and it
generates no revenue. Nothing here is for sale, no advertising is carried, and
there are no affiliate links, sponsorships, donations, or paid tiers. This is
unpaid independent writing, and saying so is part of the point: a publication
that assesses other people's claims should be clear about what it stands to
gain from them, which here is nothing.

It is not a hardware company. It is not a newsroom, and does not try to cover
everything that happens. It is not a course store, a consultancy, or a
newsletter funnel. It does not carry stock analysis, price commentary, or any
opinion at all about whether you should buy anything.

It does not cover hiring announcements, partnership announcements, contract
awards, funding rounds, or national strategy documents that contain no
technical number. Quantum sensing, quantum key distribution, and quantum
networking are outside its scope — not because they are unimportant, but
because they are different fields and pretending otherwise would make the
writing worse.

The audience is engineers and technical readers who want to know whether the
field moved or only the headline moved, and non-specialists who want one
correct frame and then only the updates that matter. Readers looking for
investment tips are in the wrong place, and the site is not going to
accommodate them.

## How results get onto the site

Automated tools are used to watch a small allowlist of sources and to flag
candidates. They are sieves. They do not report, they do not teach, and they
do not publish. Every brief on this site was read and approved by a person
before it appeared, and the Reality Board is only ever changed by hand.

After a brief is published it is frozen. If something in it turns out to be
wrong, the correction appears as a dated note at the top of that brief, and the
original text stays where it is. Silent edits would make the archive worthless.
