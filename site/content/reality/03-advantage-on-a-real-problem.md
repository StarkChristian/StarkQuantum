The qualifier is the whole cell. Advantage has been claimed on sampling tasks
chosen because they are hard for classical computers, and several of those
claims have since been narrowed by improved classical methods. Nobody needs the
output of a random circuit.

This cell asks a different question: has a quantum processor produced a result
that somebody wanted for reasons unconnected to quantum computing, and that
could not be obtained classically? A ground state energy at chemical accuracy
for a molecule beyond classical reach. A correlation function a materials group
could not otherwise get. An optimisation result a business would pay for.

The answer is no. Variational methods on current hardware remain limited by
noise and by measurement cost, and where they produce a correct answer, a
classical method generally produces it too. Laboratory advantage is not
application.
